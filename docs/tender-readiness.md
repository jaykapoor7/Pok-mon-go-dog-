# Tender readiness — the non-website work

The site can be perfect and still lose a municipal contract, because most of
what a corporation checks is paperwork that has nothing to do with the
product. This lists that paperwork so none of it is discovered late.

**This is an ops checklist, not legal advice.** Requirements vary by state and
by corporation; confirm each against the actual tender document and a CA or
company secretary before relying on it.

---

## 1. Entity and registration

- [ ] Decide and record the contracting entity (Section 8 company, registered
      society, or trust). Municipal tenders usually require the bidder to be a
      registered legal entity, not an individual or an unregistered collective.
- [ ] Certificate of incorporation / registration, with the registration number
      the tender form will ask for.
- [ ] PAN in the entity's name.
- [ ] GST registration, or a documented position on why the entity is not
      required to register. Many tenders ask for a GSTIN field with no "not
      applicable" option, so know the answer in advance.
- [ ] Bank account in the entity's name, plus a cancelled cheque — routinely
      required for EMD refunds and payment.

## 2. Tax-exemption status (affects fundraising, not eligibility)

- [ ] **12A registration** — exempts the organisation's own income.
- [ ] **80G registration** — lets Indian donors claim deduction. This is what
      most domestic donors ask for by name.
- [ ] **CSR-1 registration** with the MCA if corporate CSR funding is intended.
      Without it a company legally cannot book a CSR contribution.
- [ ] **FCRA** only if foreign contributions are intended. It is a heavy,
      separate compliance regime — do not start it casually.

## 3. Animal-welfare specific

- [ ] **AWBI recognition / ABC-implementing-agency status.** Under the Animal
      Birth Control Rules, 2023 the local body engages recognised agencies.
      Confirm the current recognition route and hold the certificate.
- [ ] Veterinary staffing evidence: registered veterinarian(s), registration
      numbers, and their association with the organisation.
- [ ] Facility evidence where the tender requires an ABC centre: premises proof,
      kennel capacity, surgical facility, and any pollution/biomedical-waste
      authorisation the state asks for.
- [ ] Biomedical waste disposal agreement, which ABC tenders commonly require.

## 4. Financial and past performance

- [ ] Audited financial statements, typically last 3 years. Start the clock
      early: a new entity cannot manufacture three years of accounts.
- [ ] Annual turnover certificate from a CA, in the format the tender specifies.
- [ ] **Past-performance dossier.** This is where StrayPaw's own register is the
      differentiator. Compile, per completed engagement:
      - ward(s) covered and the period
      - animals handled, broken down by sterilisation / vaccination / treatment
      - coverage achieved against the WHO 70% threshold
      - follow-up and outcome rates
      - the methodology note (how absence is recorded, how identity is resolved)
  Export these from the partner workspace rather than retyping them, so the
  figures in the bid match the figures in the register.
- [ ] Completion / satisfaction certificates from any prior municipal or NGO
      engagement. Ask for these at the *end of each engagement*, not years later.

## 5. Bid mechanics

- [ ] **EMD (earnest money deposit)** — know the typical amount for your target
      corporations and keep it liquid or hold a bank guarantee facility.
- [ ] **Performance security / bank guarantee** on award, commonly a percentage
      of contract value. Confirm your bank can issue one before bidding.
- [ ] **Bid capacity**: some tenders cap bids by turnover or by concurrent
      contracts. Check you qualify before spending effort on the response.
- [ ] Digital signature certificate (DSC) for the authorised signatory — most
      state e-procurement portals require it, and issuance takes days.
- [ ] Registration on the relevant e-procurement portal (state portal and/or
      GeM) well before a deadline.

## 6. Clauses to be ready for

Read these in the actual document; they decide whether a contract is survivable.

- [ ] **Payment terms and timelines**, including whether payment is per-animal,
      per-surgery, or milestone-based, and the realistic lag. Plan working
      capital for the lag, not the schedule.
- [ ] **Penalty / liquidated damages** clauses tied to targets you may not
      control (e.g. catch rates in a monsoon).
- [ ] **Data ownership.** Ensure the register's records remain available to the
      organisation and to the animals' continuity of care after the contract
      ends. This is the clause most likely to be overlooked and most damaging
      to the mission if it is lost.
- [ ] **Insurance / indemnity** requirements, including staff injury cover for
      catching teams.
- [ ] **Termination and renewal** terms.
- [ ] Anti-blacklisting declaration and any affidavit formats required.

## 7. Keep-warm list

- [ ] A single folder holding current copies of every certificate above, with
      an expiry date noted against each.
- [ ] A named person responsible for renewals.
- [ ] A short capability statement (the one-pager generated from the site) kept
      current with real register figures.
